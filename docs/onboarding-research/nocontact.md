## What's working — patterns to copy

### 1. Pre-progress-bar entry (screens 1-3)
First 3 screens have NO progress bar. They sell the vibe. Once the name is entered (screen 4), the progress bar appears with a persistent **"Free Trial"** label in the top-right. The label is on every screen of the onboarding — a constant promise/anchor that this is leading somewhere valuable but free to start.

### 2. Name as the unlock
The progress bar appears AFTER the name capture. Personalization (Aykhan, you...) then runs through the rest of the flow. Every emotional headline thereafter uses the name to make it feel custom.

### 3. Mascot as emotional cushion (Kai)
After the heavy question "What should we call you?", they break tension with a friendly mascot waving. 3 screenshots = 3 frames of one logical screen (blink → smile → personalize subtitle appears). It's a Duolingo-style move: introduce a non-human ally before asking harder questions.

### 4. Reveal-on-select for qualifier questions
"What brought you here today?" — tapping an option doesn't auto-advance. It expands into a context paragraph beneath the selection ("Breakups shock your whole system. Your brain is literally in withdrawal..."). This does two things:
- Validates the user's feeling immediately (empathy)
- Educates while they wait for the Continue button to enable

This pattern repeats on Q5 ("What do you hope to get out of...").

### 5. Multi-select vs single-select mix
- Q1 (situation): single-select with reveal
- Q2 (recency): single-select (no reveal)
- Q3 (feeling): multi-select
- Q4 (state now): multi-select
- Q5 (goal): single-select with reveal

Mixing selection modes keeps the rhythm fresh and signals "this isn't just a form."

### 6. The Authority slide
Mid-questions, slide #11: "Aykhan, you are in good hands!" with a tree diagram of Coaching Experts → AI Engineering + Behavioral Science → No Contact App. Inserted right after the heaviest emotional question. This is a credibility reset before asking the hardest commitment question (the goal duration).

### 7. Commitment escalation
Right after authority, you pick a duration. 7/14/30/60/90/180/no end date/custom. Includes an **"I'll do this later"** soft exit at the bottom — but the visual hierarchy still pushes the goal-set. The custom field is part of the bargain ("you decide").

### 8. Stat + illustration framing
After commitment, screen 13: cartoon woman on chair + cloud overhead + a stat card with a clock icon: "People without support systems take 3-4x longer to move on." Numbers + emotion + visual = perfect retention hook.

### 9. Education carousels (twice)
Two paginated horizontal scroll EDUs:
- **"It's not you, it's biology"** (4 cards): drug withdrawal analogy, emotions over logic, brain confuses loss with danger, unsolved puzzle
- **"Why No Contact is the #1 rule"** (4 cards): clearer decisions, change dynamic, break patterns, healing not reopening

Each card has its own illustration + headline + body. Page indicator dots at bottom. ONE Continue button persists. User must swipe through to learn — Continue stays alive throughout.

### 10. Comparison slide ("us vs them")
"That's why you need more than willpower" — two cards side by side: "BETTER SUPPORT / NoContact / Structure, accountability, support" vs "Other apps / One-off help." Subtle smear without naming names.

### 11. Feature reveal as PHONE-IN-PHONE mockups
Almost every feature reveal (Crisis Toolkit, Daily Tasks, Weekly Insights, Reflect, Message Analyzer, Evidence Vault, Community, Streak) shows a tilted iPhone mockup of THAT screen mid-page. Reinforces "this is real, not vaporware." Multiple feature reveals use the same template — they got 8+ screens out of one template.

### 12. Personalization loading sequence
Screens 1475-1476: 5 progress bars filling with quote at bottom. "Analyzing your situation / Setting your commitment timeline / Calibrating crisis tools / Scheduling your check-ins / Connecting you with support." Each bar a different pastel color. The visible "computing your custom plan" theater is fake but feels expensive — same trick Cal AI and Bend use.

### 13. Price anchor BEFORE paywall
Screen 1477 ("1 year cheaper than 1 hour with a human coach") — bar chart, $200+ vs No Contact. This pre-frames the eventual paywall price. By the time they ask for money, the user already knows what the comparison is.

### 14. Narrative crescendo (mountain + train)
5 screens (1478-1482) with the same illustration (mountain range with a train approaching). Only the headline changes:
- "We've Designed A Plan To Keep You On Track"
- "No Contact Isn't Just About Avoiding Your Ex - It's About Choosing Yourself"
- "You Don't Need More Discipline - You Need A System"
- "Weak Moments Happen. With Structure And Accountability, You'll Stay Strong..."
- "We've Designed A Plan To Keep You On Track" (book-ended)

CTA: "Commit to Moving On" (NOT "Continue" — the verb upgrades). Same scene, different copy = cheap to build, cinematic feel. Steals from Headspace and Bend's interlude pattern.

### 15. Plan reveal as gift
Screen 1483: "Your personalized plan is ready, Aykhan! - May 2, 2026" (the projected end date based on the goal they chose earlier). Star rating banner + chip cloud of features. CTA: "Start No Contact."

### 16. Paywall as feature carousel + testimonials in one
Screens 1484-1487: 4-page swipeable cards each with:
- A pro feature ("Heal faster", "Resist the Temptation", "Support Through Emotional Waves", "Learn the Science Behind Your Emotions")
- A 5-star rating
- A real-looking testimonial with name + age + avatar

CTA stays "Start No Contact." This is a paywall disguised as a value carousel.

### 17. Two-step paywall discount stack
- Step 1 (1488): "Exclusive Signup Offer! Get 70% OFF on No Contact - only available during sign up!" with "Claim Discount Now" centered, AND a still-visible "Start No Contact" CTA at bottom
- Step 2 (1489): "You've Unlocked Something Special!" with falling box illustration. CTA: "Open now" / soft "No Thanks"

This is a classic Cal AI / Plant Parent move: hit the user with discount panic before they bounce.

## Visual / design patterns

- **Palette**: soft purple/lavender gradient backgrounds, dark navy accents, warm beige cards. Feels calm but premium.
- **Typography**: serif headlines (looks like a custom or Tiempos-ish serif), sans body. Big emotional headlines, small body copy. 
- **Buttons**:
  - Dark navy primary buttons for emotional/commitment moments
  - White primary buttons (with dark text) for action/forward moments
  - Soft pastel purple buttons mid-flow when continuing through education
- **Progress bar**: persistent at top with circular back button on left, "Free Trial" badge top-right
- **Cards**: rounded ~20px corners, soft drop shadow, white or translucent purple fill
- **Illustrations**: custom flat illustrations (not stock). Tree mood, mountain mood, woman-on-chair mood. All same artist/style = cohesive brand.
- **Mascot**: 3D-looking purple sphere with face (Kai). Used 3-4 times across the flow as continuity anchor.

## Copy patterns to steal

- **"What brought you here today?"** — better than "Why are you using this app?"
- **"How are you doing right now, really?"** — the comma "really" adds vulnerability
- **"It's not you, it's biology"** — disarming + educational headline
- **"You don't need more discipline - you need a system"** — reframe blame to architecture
- **"Choose what feels most true to your situation"** — beats "Select one"
- **"This will take a minute. Hang tight."** — humanizes the loading screen
- **"Reset. Reflect. Refocus."** — 3-beat tagline. Echoes our positioning style.

## Anti-patterns / things to skip

- They overuse "Free Trial" in the corner from screen 4. Feels desperate by screen 20. We can be subtler — only show it after value is established.
- The mountain crescendo is 5 screens long. A bit excessive for our use case. We can compress to 2-3.
- The "Powered by AI" subtitle on the very second screen is dated. We should NOT lead with AI. Our app's emotional weight (death, legacy, sending messages from beyond) doesn't need an AI hook.
- The discount stacking at the very end (70% off then Surprise Box) feels manipulative for our category. Time-released video messages are not impulse purchases — different paywall psychology needed.

## Screen count target for FromBackInTime

NoContact ran ~36 logical screens. Our target should be **~25-30** because:
- Our user is more emotional and slower-moving than a breakup detoxer (existential stakes vs short-term coping)
- Each screen needs more breath, less density
- We can cut: AI mascot (1-2 screens), mountain crescendo (5 → 2), feature reveals (8 → 5)
- We need to ADD: trust/encryption explainer, dead-man's-switch explainer with example timeline, beneficiary/recipient picker
